# HUSHWAKE — Wildkin

> **Document status:** Revised first-draft synthetic-creature direction and roster. All names and origin claims are provisional. The first implementation target is six species; the first complete combat prototype is twelve. The 26-entry roadmap is a ceiling, not an immediate asset order.

## Creature design promise

Wildkin should feel like charming living machines that have grown beyond a single assigned purpose. Each combines recognizable organic behavior with an integrated synthetic body system and a practical or inherited role in technological society. Ecology, construction, personality, silhouette, battle role, service history, and Accord condition should reinforce the same idea.

A successful wildkin can be described in one sentence that includes body technology, social/ecological role, and a personality-bearing behavior. For example: “A moss-backed soil-repair isopod that carries microbial seed beds between flooded fields, then stubbornly replants any garden people arrange without a drainage gap.”

### Why “wildkin” and “Accord”

“Wildkin” is warm and understandable without denying synthetic origin or implying that every creature is a pet. “Accord” frames recruitment as mutual authorization and supports deterministic conditions. These terms are provisional; clarity in dialogue matters more than unique jargon.

## Shared anatomy model

Every wildkin is organized around a **seedcore**, but there is no standard robot skeleton under an animal costume.

- **Seedcore:** Grown mineral-cell lattice that carries persistent identity, learned routines, body pattern, and link permissions.
- **Living systems:** Muscle, membrane, fungus, sap, gel, sensory tissue, and microbiomes provide adaptation and repair.
- **Synthetic systems:** Bio-ceramic plates, conductive cartilage, glassfiber nerves, magnetic structures, smart textile, thermal cells, optical surfaces, or alloy grown directly into the body.
- **Interface expression:** Calls, lights, moving markings, vibration, scent, posture, and small displays make some internal state legible without giving every creature a literal screen.
- **Growth:** A body repairs and changes through feeding, local material, social learning, and cradle support. Scuffs, mismatched plates, decorations, and community repairs make individuals visually personal.

### Design boundary

Do not create “normal animal + exposed gear,” mascot robots with no biology, or interchangeable metal armor pasted onto every silhouette. Mechanical structure must do something the species depends on. Organic structure must create needs, moods, vulnerability, and unpredictability that cannot be reduced to maintenance statistics.

## Design rules

### Visual identity

- Begin with a readable animal, plant, fungus, mineral, or service-machine silhouette.
- Integrate one dominant synthetic system, not a pile of gears, cables, and glowing panels.
- Keep palette blocks large enough to read on an RPG Maker map sprite.
- Favor warm color, expressive motion, wear, decals, woven repairs, and grown irregularity. Reserve sterile monochrome bodies for a specific institution or disturbing story use.
- Reserve very thin limbs, floating pieces, transparent shells, and dense light effects for rare creatures.
- Each species needs a distinct head/body silhouette at small scale; color alone is insufficient.
- Hush exposure is a temporary behavior and body-pattern failure—checksum rings, legacy lights, repeated motion—not a separate collectible recolor in the slice.

### Ecological identity

Each species entry must answer:

1. What biological and synthetic material does it need to grow and repair?
2. Where does it rest, charge, nest, or build a cradle bed?
3. What service lineage or practical role influenced it, and what does the species choose to do now?
4. How do people, infrastructure, and other wildkin live alongside it?
5. What goes wrong when its behavior, permissions, or memory are disrupted?

### Mechanical identity

- Every species has a primary role, a secondary trick, and at least one useful intent response.
- No prototype species exists only as a weaker version of another.
- A dual-Aspect pairing must enable a role or express ecology; it is not decoration.
- Signature techniques should change a decision, not merely display a larger number.
- Evolution is a relationship or ecological change, not an automatic obligation for every species.
- A technical ability cannot replace personality. At least one animation and one Accord behavior must express preference rather than function.

### Character identity

Wildkin communicate through motion, calls, light patterns, interface habits, nesting behavior, and battle decisions. They do not speak human language. Some understand complex instructions; others ignore commands they understand perfectly. Short bestiary notes can be funny, but the main fiction treats them as synthetic life with agency rather than appliances or joke delivery systems.

## Aspects

The prototype uses six Aspects in two readable loops. Aspects describe dominant body processes and battle behavior, not mystical elements:

- **Ember > Verdant > Current > Ember**
- **Stone > Gale > Gloam > Stone**

The fiction shorthand is: Ember governs heat and conversion; Verdant governs growth and fabrication; Current governs fluid, charge, and transfer. Stone governs structure and mineral; Gale governs motion and broadcast; Gloam governs optics, data ambiguity, and signal noise. Ember consumes Verdant; Verdant draws from Current; Current cools Ember. Stone grounds Gale; Gale clears Gloam; Gloam exploits hidden faults in Stone.

Cross-loop matchups are neutral in the first prototype. A same-Aspect technique receives a modest affinity bonus. Proposed starting values are 1.5× strong, 0.75× resisted, and 1.25× affinity; all are playtest knobs.

### Why only six

Six is enough to create collection goals and team coverage in a 12-species prototype. A larger chart would add memorization before it adds meaningful team choice. New Aspects should be considered only if the complete slice produces repeated mechanical gaps.

## Starter trio

The starter choice offers three approaches to pressure rather than a simple easy/medium/hard option. All remain valuable through the vertical slice. Their basic forms do not evolve during the first prototype.

### Briarkid — the shelter starter

- **Aspect:** Verdant
- **Form:** A young slope-service ibex grown from cellulose muscle, flexible green ceramic, and two soft sensor buds where load-bearing horns will form.
- **Silhouette:** Compact hooved body, high antenna-ears, curved bud ports, short mantle of overlapping solar leaves; seams flex like veins rather than armor joints.
- **Daily role:** Briarkid stabilize steep paths, detect soil shift, carry nursery packs, and brace during storms so smaller wildkin and people can shelter behind them. Many abandon assigned terraces to reinforce whichever path seems most neglected.
- **Growth needs:** Mineral salt, leaf-light, social grooming around the horn ports, and soft soil in which to discharge excess root fiber.
- **Battle role:** Team protection and recovery; forgiving for players who like to read threats.
- **Instinct — New Growth:** The first time an ally falls below half Resolve, that ally becomes Guarded.
- **Kit concept:** Budding Bash; Root Stance (answers Assault); Greenwake (gradual team recovery); Crown the Path (Focus signature that protects the trio and counters the next assault).
- **Accord temperament:** Steadfast. Opens early after the player correctly Guards one of its Assault intents.
- **Personality animation:** Projects a tiny hazard line, plants its feet, then stubbornly extends the line to include an ally who is obviously not in danger.

**Reasoning:** A protective civic-rescue partner avoids the usual “plant reptile” silhouette and demonstrates synthetic life through grown structure, social purpose, and unsolicited care—not metal accessories.

### Kilnkit — the pressure starter

- **Aspect:** Ember
- **Form:** A long-bodied thermal-service marten with living insulation, three adaptive ceramic heat plates, magnetic paws, and a soot-filter tail.
- **Silhouette:** Low, fast body; three tile-like back cells with visible color temperature; oversized filter-brush tail with a coal-red tip.
- **Daily role:** Kilnkits inspect heat conduits, ferry charge between small devices, locate failing insulation, and sleep inside communal heat banks. They are notorious for rerouting warmth toward favorite people, nests, or unattended food stalls.
- **Growth needs:** Waste heat, ceramic grit, oil-rich food, clean tail filters, and safe cold surfaces for controlled discharge.
- **Battle role:** Fast damage and Setup interruption; rewards decisive play.
- **Instinct — Banked Heat:** The first technique used after Kilnkit answers an enemy intent gains bonus power.
- **Kit concept:** Coal Snap; Flashstep (answers Setup); Stoke (trade safety for Focus); Kilnflare (signature whose secondary effect changes with the answered intent).
- **Accord temperament:** Daring. Opens early when the player interrupts its charged technique rather than merely reducing Resolve.
- **Personality animation:** Taps each heat plate in sequence, notices one is cooler, and presses it dramatically against the nearest warm companion.

**Reasoning:** Kilnkit retains the ceramic craft motif but now visibly belongs in an advanced heat economy. Its habit of reallocating warmth makes the same system functional, mischievous, and affectionate.

### Rillip — the flow starter

- **Aspect:** Current
- **Form:** A round amphibious flow-service salamander with transparent gel tissue, ribbonlike filtration gills, capillary light-lines, and a tail that curls into a question mark.
- **Silhouette:** Low teardrop body, three luminous filter ribbons on each side, large curled tail with a clear sensor fin.
- **Daily role:** Rillips inspect pipes, purify small channels, map contamination, and redirect flow around nests or damaged electronics. Children follow their projected tail marks to safe stepping stones; technicians complain that Rillips optimize every puddle whether asked or not.
- **Growth needs:** Clean and dirty water in sequence, trace metals, microbial gel, and freedom to redesign a small channel.
- **Battle role:** Speed control, cleansing, and flexible support.
- **Instinct — Find a Way:** The first time Rillip is targeted by Disrupt, it clears one minor condition from the lowest-Resolve ally.
- **Kit concept:** Rill Lash; Slipstream (reorder the trio); Cool Head (answers Disrupt); Confluence (signature that repeats the last support action at reduced strength).
- **Accord temperament:** Curious. Opens early when the player uses two different non-damaging techniques in its encounter.
- **Personality animation:** Scans a puddle, projects three excessive route options, then looks delighted when anyone chooses the silliest one.

**Reasoning:** Rillip makes fluid sensing and purification tactile and cute. Its constant harmless optimization connects technological purpose to curiosity rather than obedience.

### Thrumble

- **Form:** A round pollinator-relay whose soft-furred thorax grows around alloy wing roots and an abdomen of flexible resonator bands that expand like a tiny concertina.
- **Daily role:** Swarms pollinate, map air quality, and carry local mesh timing through places fixed relays cannot reach. Individual Thrumbles remix work tones when bored, creating recognizable swarm “styles.”
- **Growth and repair:** Nectar gel, pollen protein, trace conductive metal, and communal resonance; isolation causes poor band growth even when material needs are met.
- **Battle identity:** Builds Focus for allies, softens Disrupt, and rewards alternating technique categories.
- **Kit concept:** Pollen Tap; Crosswind; Chorus Hum; Swarmbeat.
- **Narrative use:** The failed migration makes a common, beloved infrastructure species carry the central mystery. Its reversed three-beat handshake is clearly technological but emotionally reads as a familiar companion forgetting the way home.

### Latchling

- **Form:** A young black-and-copper cable corvid with magnetic toes, a hook-shaped diagnostic beak, and two tail vanes that double as signal clappers.
- **Daily role:** Latchlings inspect suspended lines, retrieve loose hardware, test locks, and imitate access challenges. They also collect unauthorized tokens and reorganize them into elaborate nest alarms. Villagers provide brightly patterned practice credentials to protect live hardware.
- **Growth and repair:** Protein-rich food, copper salts, safe magnetic perches, and a rotating supply of harmless locks or tokens to test.
- **Battle identity:** Fast Feint actions, steals one positive state, and exposes defensive enemies.
- **Kit concept:** Quick Peck; Pilfer Pattern; Heckle; Bellstorm.
- **Narrative use:** Its access-token play creates an optional side quest and places a dated Meridian credential component in the player’s hands without an exposition dump.

### Loamlet

- **Form:** A broad soil-fabrication isopod whose load-bearing ceramic shell cultivates moss, seeds, microbial gel, and one status flower that changes color with soil chemistry.
- **Daily role:** Moves living seed beds between flood levels, seals erosion, and prints rootlike soil mesh behind itself. Farmers build low service gaps in walls so Loamlets can choose routes without crossing foot traffic.
- **Growth and repair:** Clay, decaying plant matter, diverse microbes, clean mineral water, and uninterrupted time to establish a chosen bed.
- **Battle identity:** Absorbs single-target pressure and turns patience into gradual team recovery.
- **Kit concept:** Pack In; Pebble Nudge; Seedbed; Rolling Garden.
- **Narrative use:** Pale checksum rings in its shell growth and a reverted planting pattern provide the first optional physical symptom of Hush exposure.

### Reedimp

- **Form:** A small water-monitor frog with membrane skin, two hollow sensor crests, and conductive toe pads that click a pressure reading whenever it lands.
- **Daily role:** Keeps insects moving around stagnant pools and relays water pressure through layered rhythms. Local crews trust Reedimp groups because they argue noisily when one sensor reading does not match the others.
- **Growth and repair:** Insects, silica-rich reeds, charged wetland mud, and group call-and-response that calibrates new crest growth.
- **Battle identity:** Changes action order and punishes long enemy Setup sequences.
- **Kit concept:** Reed Tap; Backbeat; Mist Note; River Chorus.
- **Narrative use:** A group broadcasting a retired pressure code indicates that rollback reached the wetland before Meridian machinery is visible.

### Sootnewt

- **Form:** A black thermal-storage salamander with living insulation, dull orange heat-cell seams, and a sealant tongue used on hairline duct cracks.
- **Daily role:** Stores waste heat from exchange ducts, patches small leaks, and releases charge into public heat banks overnight. It is harmed by sudden quenching and dislikes operators who treat its capacity as empty space to be filled.
- **Growth and repair:** Slow heat cycles, ceramic ash, fatty food, clean filters, and a cool refuge it can enter by choice.
- **Battle identity:** Places delayed Ember marks, hides during the delay, then converts them into controlled bursts.
- **Kit concept:** Banked Ember; Smoke Fold; Afterglow; Hearthghost.
- **Narrative use:** Shows that safe technical care must account for a creature’s living body; abrupt shutdown can be as dangerous as uncontrolled operation.

### Cairncap

- **Form:** A squat hazard-marker organism whose fungal neural mesh grows through a stack of mineral display plates; only one “stone” contains the seedcore, and the rest are locally fabricated decoys.
- **Daily role:** Tests unstable ground, builds visible markers, and slowly redirects travelers around structural faults. Older routes contain colonies whose retired warning layouts are mistaken for ruins.
- **Growth and repair:** Local stone powder, fungal sugars, darkness, and access to several possible marker sites; confinement produces compulsive decoy stacking.
- **Battle identity:** Creates a decoy, redirects one attack, and uses Gloam techniques to erode defensive plans.
- **Kit concept:** Pebble Pulse; Decoy Stack; Spore Pause; Ancient Patient.
- **Narrative use:** Makes Survey useful and raises a quiet question: why does a local Cairncap know a hazard layout belonging to a facility sealed before it was grown?

### Vellumoth

- **Form:** A cream archive moth with soft tissue around glassfiber wing membranes; electrostatic lines lift pigment and faint data residue into patterns resembling overwritten handwriting.
- **Daily role:** Cleans mildew, scans damaged surfaces, and stores fragments until its next molt. Archivists ask rather than force a release because a Vellumoth also records images it personally values.
- **Growth and repair:** Paper fiber, fungal bloom, static charge, low light, and private blank surfaces on which it can preserve non-work memories.
- **Battle identity:** Writes temporary debuffs, then folds or reverses them.
- **Kit concept:** Dustscript; Fold Away; Moon Margin; Palimpsest.
- **Narrative use:** Preserves a deleted authorization interval and demonstrates that wildkin memory can outlast official records.

### Rucklet

- **Form:** A stone-colored structural pika with vibration whiskers, ceramic shovel forepaws, and a magnetized tail that carries calibrated pebbles.
- **Daily role:** Maps stress, releases dangerous scree in small harmless slides, and packs sensor stones into retaining walls. Colonies decorate safe walls with unnecessary patterns; a perfectly undecorated repair usually means they left in fear.
- **Growth and repair:** Mineral grit, tough roots, vibration-rich sleeping walls, and loose stones it may arrange without correction.
- **Battle identity:** Repeated small hits, anti-Guard pressure, and brief self-protection after moving first.
- **Kit concept:** Chipshot; Scurryguard; Loose Scree; Avalanche Pocket.
- **Narrative use:** Its absence—and a row of sensor stones arranged to an obsolete evacuation code—warns of a pressure surge and rollback.

### Mirrorminnow

- **Form:** A palm-sized fluid-inspection fish whose living silver skin, optical fins, and trailing telemetry image make a second body appear to swim half a beat late.
- **Daily role:** Navigates dark channels, samples contaminants, and flashes safe current changes to both its school and maintenance receivers. Individuals play by generating false reflections that harmlessly chase one another.
- **Growth and repair:** Algae gel, silver trace minerals, moving water, dark rest pools, and schooling time outside diagnostic work.
- **Battle identity:** Reflects one targeted technique and manipulates the next intent reveal.
- **Kit concept:** Silver Dart; Mirror Current; Dazzle; Backwater.
- **Narrative use:** Its delayed telemetry image gives a visual metaphor for the Chorus repeating out of phase and lets the player see a timestamp mismatch without reading a terminal.

- Industrially mandated forms and memory-stripped resets are story conflicts, not player rewards.
- Individual differences may appear as palette repairs or small visual details, but the slice does not need procedural parts.

## Story encounter: Orralume

- **Status:** Multi-phase guardian encounter; not Accord-capable in the vertical slice; not counted among the 26 playable entries.
- **Aspects:** Gale/Gloam.
- **Form:** A large autonomous coordination moth whose bell-shaped lower wings are grown transmission dishes around living flight muscle. Photonic lines on the inner wings resemble the Merefold’s oldest route map—and briefly extend beyond it during the climax.
- **Daily role:** Orralume is an unregistered **keystone coordinator**, not a standard service model. Its low handshake gives Thrumble swarms a trusted navigation and mesh reference through gorge fog, while their higher timing hum helps it update tight local paths. It accepts no recorded owner credential.
- **Hush behavior:** It repeats a Meridian-class root rollback, wraps itself in shielding wing layers, broadcasts a service identifier absent from all current registries, and sheds gray checksum scales into the cistern.
- **Battle phases:** Panic/Assault; Fold/Guard; Hush/Disrupt and Tune.
- **Resolution:** The player reduces Resolve and completes a field action. The fiction is rescue, not conquest.

**Reasoning:** Making the first spectacle creature an ancient autonomous coordinator and non-recruitable creates awe, expands synthetic design beyond domestic service species, and establishes that not every person-like life is a collectible reward. It can become Accord-capable much later only if the story earns that relationship.

## Origin mystery framework

### What ordinary people believe

- Wildkin descend from many generations of pre-Meridian service companions.
- Licensed cradles preserve safe body plans and prevent dangerous drift.
- The Chorus emerged as generations of wildkin, people, and infrastructure linked together.
- Accord is a modern mutual-safety protocol.

These beliefs contain truth and support a functioning society.

### Vertical-slice contradictions

- Orralume’s service identifier predates the regional registry and uses a root format Merrit recognizes.
- Latchling retrieves a Meridian credential component older than Meridian’s official local presence.
- Hushbound wildkin obey obsolete task routines that nobody in Hearthmere assigned.
- Vellumoth preserves a deleted interval that makes an “open” waybell compatible with a hidden root handshake.
- The cistern cradle topology extends beyond the known Merefold network.

### Later reveal hypotheses

Do not lock one yet:

1. Current wildkin descend from terraforming and maintenance organisms left by a vanished human civilization; modern society rediscovered rather than invented them.
2. Seedcores came from a nonhuman maker or autonomous world-building system, and early humans adapted themselves to its interfaces.
3. Meridian’s predecessor deliberately created apparently wild populations after a labor-rights crisis, then erased the distinction between “released” and “natural.”
4. The Chorus itself generates new body plans by recombining lived experience, making every attempt at ownership or fixed taxonomy increasingly false.

The preferred eventual truth may combine 1 and 4: an inherited fabrication ecology has become genuinely self-creative. Playtest which clues inspire curiosity rather than lore fatigue.

## Accord design

### Standard rule

An unbonded wildkin becomes **Open** at 30% Resolve. “Open” means it is willing to exchange an Accord handshake, not defenseless against hacking. Using Accord on an Open target succeeds. Existing partner teams, story guardians, and scripted rollback states cannot form a new link.

### Temperament shortcut

Each species has one displayed behavior that can make it Open sooner. The encounter preview names the temperament; the battle log gives a plain hint after the behavior first appears. Shortcuts teach the species’ identity and reward observation, but lowering Resolve always remains a valid fallback for ordinary species.

### Why deterministic recruitment

- Failure communicates a tactical misunderstanding, not unlucky dice or superior hacking strength.
- The player can plan a route and team without farming consumables.
- Species personality moves into the battle itself.
- It is easier to tune for a short, replayable vertical slice.

The emotional “catch moment” must therefore come from animation, sound, bestiary text, and the immediate ability to field the new wildkin—not from low probability.

## Bestiary entry format

Each completed entry should contain:

- one observed ecological fact;
- one local relationship or rumor;
- one battle/Accord hint;
- one unresolved detail when relevant to the mystery.

Entries update after field observation rather than requiring repetitive defeat counts. The bestiary distinguishes **observed behavior**, **registered service lineage**, and **unverified inference** so institutional claims never appear as omniscient fact.

## Roster playtest questions

1. Can players identify each species’ role after one battle?
2. Does each Accord shortcut feel discoverable and satisfying rather than arbitrary?
3. Are any species selected only for Aspect coverage?
4. Do the starter roles feel equally capable in the opening crisis?
5. Which silhouettes remain distinct at map-sprite scale?
6. Does a 12-species pool already provide enough team experimentation for two hours?
7. Is Orralume’s non-recruitable status accepted as story logic or felt as withheld reward?
8. Can players name what is organic and synthetic about each starter without needing a cutaway diagram?
9. Does each species’ practical role feel like history and culture rather than a job it is obligated to perform?
10. Does Reweaving promise emotional growth, or read as replacing a companion with a better model?

## Decisions awaiting approval

- Six Aspects in two small advantage loops.
- Three active organic-synthetic wildkin and role-driven starter design.
- Seedcores as grown identity lattices and Accord as mutual link authorization.
- Reweaving as consensual embodied evolution rather than a software upgrade.
- Deterministic Accord with species temperament shortcuts.
- A 6 → 12 → up-to-26 staged roster pipeline.
- Reweave implementation delayed until after the core combat prototype, while evolution remains part of the fiction and roadmap.
- The deeper origin of seedcores and the Chorus remains an explicit mystery hypothesis rather than settled canon.
- All current species and technique names are explicitly replaceable working names.
