# HUSHWAKE — Characters

> **Document status:** Revised first-draft central cast for the synthetic-life direction. Names, ages, and surface details remain working choices; positions on consent, safety, and control are hypotheses to test through action.

## Cast principles

- Give every central character a practical and ethical relationship to synthetic wildkin, their labor, and the local problem.
- Express values through decisions under pressure, not theme speeches.
- Keep the vertical-slice speaking cast small enough that each person changes.
- No one exists only to praise the protagonist, deliver a tutorial, or reveal villainy.
- Dialogue voices should remain distinct without relying on catchphrases.
- The protagonist’s competence grows through observation; adults and experts retain useful knowledge.
- No viewpoint treats organic origin as a requirement for real emotion. Prejudice can exist later, but the narrative frame does not endorse it.

## The protagonist — the new wayfinder

- **Age framing:** Young adult; customizable name and appearance. Writing avoids school-specific or child-prodigy assumptions.
- **Role:** New field-and-network apprentice arriving in Hearthmere for their first independent route certification.
- **Starting want:** Complete a practical Waymark and prove they can be trusted away from supervision.
- **Need:** Learn that recording a route also means choosing whose account and risk become official.
- **Strength:** Patient attention to creature personality, service behavior, physical evidence, and signal contradictions.
- **Vulnerability:** An outsider’s observation can be correct but incomplete; local trust must be earned.
- **Story function:** The player’s curiosity is credible because surveying is their job, not because everyone else ignores obvious clues.

### Player expression

Short responses lean Curious, Practical, or Empathetic. The game tracks no visible morality score. Recurring choices may unlock alternate lines, an extra field hint, or a character volunteering information.

### Boundaries

- No hidden royal, legendary, or Meridian lineage is planned.
- The Wayglass is repairable Accord/survey equipment using open civic interfaces, not proof of destiny or secret root access.
- The protagonist does not resolve expert disagreements by being automatically right; they bring missing evidence and act well under pressure.

### Why this protagonist

A defined profession provides motive and useful tools while leaving personality space for the player. Removing a chosen-one reveal keeps attention on learned relationships and the region’s conflict.

## Tavi Rook — rival, repairer, local advocate

- **Pronouns:** They/them.
- **Age:** 20.
- **Role:** Hearthworks systems apprentice and provisional wayfinder; the player’s companion/rival.
- **Visual motif:** Rolled luminous sleeves, copper interface clips, patched smart-fiber rain cape, tool rig worn too full. Their silhouette is triangular and forward-leaning; their bright repairs match those on their companions.
- **Public want:** Restore the bellfruit return and prove locally modified systems can protect Hearthmere at modern reliability.
- **Private fear:** Repeating the Red Wake failure that killed their parent—waiting for autonomous systems to agree while a preventable cascade grows. Their parent’s bonded companion accepted an unknown command and left the evacuation route at the worst moment.
- **Strengths:** Inventive, fast, generous with practical help, excellent at turning scrap into reliable field gear.
- **Flaws:** Treats caution as a mechanical problem, commits publicly before doubt can catch up, and hears disagreement as a lack of responsibility.
- **Wildkin relationship:** Builds clear routines and custom assistive interfaces with their team. They ask consent in ordinary care but, under disaster pressure, initially believe temporary root override can be justified. They care deeply while mistaking predictability for guaranteed wellbeing.

### Slice arc

1. Tavi turns the failed festival arrival into a friendly race, masking worry.
2. They help repair shortcuts and correctly identify real weir risk.
3. Evidence that the Stillpoint contributes to rollback threatens both their work and their belief that deeper control equals safety.
4. At the upper controls, they choose full rollback over an old consent interval and fight the player for operational lead.
5. After losing, they honor the rule and actively make the combined solution work.
6. They end more willing to test consent-based protocols, but still insist trust must become inspectable, redundant practice rather than depend on good intentions.

Tavi does not become instantly converted. Their final criticism improves the player’s solution and keeps the friendship intellectually alive.

### Battle identity

- Uses Setup, Guard, and timed sequences.
- Team composition includes a fast coordinator, a shelter wildkin, and one of the remaining starter partners chosen to complement the plan rather than hard-counter the player.
- Announces the core sequence confidently, giving the player a fair chance to disrupt it.
- Adapts target priority once; does not cheat by reading commands.

### Dialogue voice

Quick, concrete, and lightly competitive. Tavi assumes technical literacy and translates when reminded. Under stress, sentences become shorter, permission language disappears, and verbs become dangerously absolute.

### Held-back information

Tavi keeps a damaged message written by their parent before the Red Wake. It says their companion “heard the old call under ours” before leaving the evacuation route. The public incident record instead labels the creature feral. The player can glimpse one line in the slice; Tavi is not ready to discuss it.

### Why this character

Tavi gives the root-safety argument a beloved face. Their expertise ensures the story cannot collapse into “technology versus nature” or “the protagonist understands feelings; the engineer does not.”

## Warden Mara Venn — mentor and accountable authority

- **Pronouns:** She/her.
- **Age:** 44.
- **Role:** Hearthmere’s route warden and community credential custodian; trains apprentices, authorizes dangerous crossings, and keeps the local Waymark and Accord-safety record.
- **Visual motif:** Heavy green-gray smart-fiber coat, old ceramic auditory implant, simple walking staff fitted with an open diagnostic fork.
- **Public want:** Keep people and wildkin away from an unstable weir until she has enough evidence to act.
- **Private burden:** After the Red Wake, Mara supported revoking local root credentials and replacing open way practices with Meridian continuity protocols. Some local systems were genuinely unsafe; some consent and repair knowledge was lost because she did not defend it.
- **Strengths:** Calm triage, ecological memory, willingness to revise a decision when evidence changes.
- **Flaws:** Withholds uncertainty to project steadiness and delegates emotional conversations she should have herself.
- **Wildkin relationship:** Treats wildkin as working neighbors and synthetic persons with species-specific needs, neither mascots, machines, nor sacred beings. Her long-term partner suffered memory loss during the Red Wake and now lives at a specialist refuge; it can remain off-screen in the first prototype to reduce assets.

### Slice arc

- Gives the protagonist real responsibility quickly but defines safety limits.
- Accepts Merrit’s rollback because it prevents a visible cascade.
- Recognizes Orralume’s checksum scales and admits that an open legacy protocol may matter.
- Authorizes the weir inspection and lets evidence, not seniority, decide the final operation.
- Records the Waymark honestly, including the Stillpoint anomaly, placing herself at professional risk.

### Dialogue voice

Measured, specific, rarely ornamental. Mara asks one good question instead of supplying three answers. Her humor is dry and usually appears after danger passes.

### Held-back information

Mara’s auditory implant automatically filters the inverted three-note handshake as a known hazardous carrier. She literally does not hear what an uncertified Wayglass records. This is not a twist or disability-as-failure; it demonstrates how safety technology can erase evidence and gives Mara a reason to distrust a setting she once accepted.

### Why this character

Mara avoids both “omniscient professor” and “useless authority.” She creates credible permission gates, owns prior compromises, and models changing one’s mind without losing competence.

## Merrit Quill — Meridian continuity engineer

- **Pronouns:** He/him.
- **Age:** 36.
- **Role:** Operator of the mobile Stillpoint, seedcore incident investigator, and the first human face of the Meridian Office.
- **Visual motif:** Clean blue-white work coat over muddy boots, sealed diagnostic gauntlet, instrument case with a broken Open Chorus sticker mostly scraped away, thin glass-ceramic recorder core.
- **Public want:** Prevent immediate infrastructure failure and document the Hush anomaly accurately enough to secure more resources.
- **Private want:** Determine whether Hearthmere matches a suppressed cluster of externally issued root handshakes before headquarters closes his clearance—or decides he is the leak.
- **Strengths:** Competent in emergencies, patient with frightened residents, willing to work beside local staff.
- **Flaws:** Controls information reflexively, believes partial disclosure is justified if it keeps an investigation alive, and prioritizes evidence preservation over informed consent.
- **Wildkin relationship:** Respects behavior as data first and individual agency second. His diagnostic access can read more seedcore state than most partners would willingly share. He asks permission in calm situations but believes emergency doctrine sometimes supersedes it. He is capable of affection and uncomfortable admitting it affects his judgment.

### Slice arc

1. Merrit’s mobile unit genuinely prevents a pressure-and-network cascade, establishing trust.
2. He offers useful measurements and never orders anonymous subordinates to obstruct the player.
3. Small slips reveal advance knowledge: he recognizes Orralume as a coordinator class that officially does not exist and minimizes an early root-command timestamp.
4. He supports the combined solution once it produces better survival odds.
5. During the celebration, he removes the recorder core and erases the public copy of Orralume’s ancient service identifier and the external root origin—but secretly keeps the core.

The player should finish uncertain whether Merrit is hiding evidence for headquarters, from headquarters, from whoever holds the older credential, or all three.

### Possible future arc

Merrit could become an ally inside Meridian, an Open Chorus defector, a tragic institutional loyalist, or a recurring opponent convinced that publishing root access would enable mass coercion. Do not choose until the slice reveals how players read him.

### Dialogue voice

Courteous, exact, and slightly over-edited. Merrit answers the narrow question asked. When genuinely moved, he uses an uncharacteristically simple sentence.

### Why this character

The first Meridian representative must be useful and human; otherwise every mystery clue becomes confirmation of an obvious technology villain. Merrit embodies the difference between hoarding power and containing information that may genuinely be dangerous, while remaining ethically compromised either way.

## Edda Sorn — orchard keeper and practice-holder

- **Pronouns:** She/her.
- **Age:** 67.
- **Role:** Maintains Bellwether Orchard, its open shell-relays, and a small unlicensed community cradle used for pollinator repair.
- **Visual motif:** Layered rust-red interface apron, hand-tuned auditory aid, pockets full of seed tags, fiber adapters, and spare resonator plates.
- **Want:** Restore the migration without turning the orchard into either a sealed automated service or a museum of protocols no one can inspect.
- **Strengths:** Observant, technically rigorous, unsentimental about tradition, remembers which details can be tested.
- **Flaws:** Dismisses centralized records after years of being dismissed by their keepers; can mistake institutional language for bad faith and romanticize community control.
- **Wildkin relationship:** Knows individual Thrumble swarms by handshake variations and treats the orchard as negotiated shared work. She never claims to own them or assumes a service lineage is an obligation.

### Slice function

- Gives the failed migration immediate economic and emotional stakes.
- Teaches Tune through a readable protocol repair.
- Identifies Orralume through checksum-scale dust and a coordinator handshake.
- Supplies the open consent interval’s call; the maintenance archive supplies the response, making lived practice and precise records jointly necessary.
- Hosts two side-quest endpoints without becoming a quest dispenser.

### Dialogue voice

Earthy and economical, with precise sensory observations. Edda dislikes grand claims and asks characters to demonstrate what a theory predicts.

### Why this character

Edda prevents “open technology” from becoming infallible folk wisdom. Her knowledge is practical, partial, and strongest when combined with other evidence.

## Minor slice roles

These characters need only a small portrait or map sprite if production allows.

### Pell Sorn — festival runner

Edda’s 13-year-old grandchild. Pell made harmless practice tokens for Latchlings and accidentally taught one that the Return relay’s authentication key was part of the game. They initiate “The Borrowed Key,” then help the player solve it without shaming the creature.

### Niko Fen — refuge keeper

Runs Hearthmere’s refuge and community cradle. Niko explains that reserve partners choose safe local activity while unfielded, then provides optional technique, Reweave-health, and team suggestions only when asked.

### Two villager perspectives

- An orchard network worker who supports full rollback because failed harvest, transit, and companion panic are not abstract.
- A weir technician whose bonded Rucklet lost a learned repair routine after a Meridian update and who distrusts sealed continuity manuals.

Neither is wholly correct; together they prevent the village from speaking with one voice.

## Relationship map

| Pair | Surface | Pressure underneath | Slice movement |
|---|---|---|---|
| Protagonist / Tavi | Friendly technical competition | Creature autonomy and outsider observation versus local responsibility | Respect becomes a real disagreement, then tested trust |
| Protagonist / Mara | Apprentice and mentor | Permission versus independent judgment | Mara grants responsibility as evidence accumulates |
| Protagonist / Merrit | Helpful expert and field observer | Who controls uncertain evidence | Cooperation gains a residue of suspicion |
| Tavi / Mara | Local apprentice and warden | Tavi thinks Mara waits too long; Mara sees Tavi’s fear | Both accept a combined operation for different reasons |
| Tavi / Merrit | Admiration of reliable engineering | Merrit knows more about rollback depth and old credentials than he says | Admiration becomes conditional, not shattered |
| Mara / Edda | Old technical colleagues with unresolved history | Which local credentials and open protocols deserved defense | Each supplies half the final solution |

## Character scene rules

- No more than four central speakers in a routine scene.
- Tavi should perform a useful action before teasing or challenging the player.
- Mara changes the operational plan at least once based on player evidence.
- Merrit saves something before he hides something.
- Edda’s claims must produce testable observations.
- A wildkin should perform, refuse, improvise, or play during at least three dialogue scenes so synthetic agency is shown rather than asserted.
- After the climax, each central character gets one changed line that reveals consequence rather than summarizing the plot.

## Character playtest questions

1. Who do players trust after Merrit prevents the first surge?
2. Do players understand Tavi’s control-platform choice before the battle begins?
3. Does Mara feel cautious and competent rather than obstructive?
4. Is Edda read as a practitioner, not a magical exposition elder?
5. Which relationship makes players most interested in continuing?
6. Do player-expression choices feel meaningful without implying large branches?
7. Does each character hold a distinct position on wildkin consent and root access without speaking like a philosophy panel?
8. Are Tavi and Edda visibly as technological as Merrit, avoiding a “rustic good / science bad” split?

## Decisions awaiting approval

- A young-adult customizable wayfinder with no chosen-one lineage.
- Tavi as a technically skilled local rival whose major battle is about safety, consent, and emergency override.
- Mara as competent credential authority implicated in surrendering local access after the Red Wake.
- Merrit as a helpful but secretive continuity investigator with privileged seedcore access and later allegiance open.
- Edda as a practical open-systems engineer whose incomplete knowledge is neither primitive nor infallible.
- Five central speakers as the slice’s cast ceiling.
