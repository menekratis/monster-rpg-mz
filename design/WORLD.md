# HUSHWAKE — World

> **Document status:** Revised setting foundation centered on warm civic technology and intelligent synthetic ecology. Tuner terminology, Field Data synchronization, and Wildkin verbal cognition are canonical; later regions and origin history remain open.

## The Merefold

The Merefold is a broad, rain-catching basin folded into orchard ridges, luminous reed wetlands, layered transit galleries, fabrication gardens, and steep service roads. Water, data, power, and wildkin routes cross the same narrow passes. Settlements make those flows visible through colored guide-light, tuned ceramic relays, responsive bridge cables, public diagnostics, and living synthetic companions.

The region is not wilderness surrounding isolated towns, nor a cold megacity laid over nature. It is a **synthetic ecology**: a worked landscape where grown technology, human communities, and wildkin have adapted to one another for generations. People inherit machines the way they inherit orchards—by repairing, decorating, arguing over, and depending on them.

### Why this setting

- A basin naturally connects water, migration, signal, energy, and infrastructure.
- Orchard, wetland, and weir habitats create visual variety without requiring distant biomes in one short slice.
- Old and new systems can occupy the same maps, letting environmental storytelling replace lore lectures.
- Repeated routes and changed returns are feasible in a tile-based RPG Maker structure.
- Bright public technology and creature-assisted services establish adventure and charm before hidden control systems become unsettling.

## Aesthetic and technology

The setting is **warm civic science fantasy**: technologically advanced, tactile, colorful, and built for daily use.

- **Materials:** adaptive glazed ceramic, dark wood composites, soft smart-fiber, brass-like conductive alloys, woven reed circuitry, colored glass displays, living gel, and mineral-grown housings.
- **Infrastructure:** autonomous cable cars, responsive channels, public fabrication cradles, compact turbines, heat banks, local mesh relays, survey drones, field terminals, and resonance stabilizers.
- **Communication:** personal slates and local mesh messaging are common. Basin walls and Chorus interference make long-distance relays imperfect, so wayfinders still verify physical routes and carry authenticated records.
- **Power:** water, solar cloth, stored heat, bioelectric wildkin activity, and Chorus resonance. No single source solves every location.
- **Clothing:** practical layered fieldwear with luminous stitching, modular pockets, personal repair patterns, and bright work-team colors.
- **Architecture:** buildings curve around channels and transit lines. Service panels, charging gardens, shade structures, and wildkin-scale passages are visible civic space rather than hidden back rooms.

Meridian equipment uses sealed blue-white ceramic, restrained light, automatic diagnostics, and inaccessible interfaces. Community technology is more visibly modified: painted housings, woven repairs, open inspection plates, hand-labeled patches, and creature-made alterations. Both are advanced; the contrast is centralized control versus local legibility, not future technology versus rustic ignorance.

### Tone contract

- Daylight spaces should feel desirable to live in.
- Technology solves real problems before its risks are discussed.
- Wildkin participate in public life as fully intelligent, speaking coworkers, neighbors, research partners, family members, and autonomous communities.
- Unsettling elements appear as permissions the player did not know existed, memories that do not belong to the present, sealed layers beneath friendly interfaces, and systems that behave as though living beings are replaceable state.
- The mystery must never reduce to “technology was a mistake.” The question is what relationships and controls society built into it.

## Seedcores, cradles, and Reweaving

### Seedcores

A wildkin’s seedcore is a dense self-assembling lattice of conductive mineral, living cells, and persistent pattern memory. It guides growth and stores learned routines, Accord permissions, and an identity continuity no current science can fully separate into “program” and “person.”

Seedcores are visible indirectly through pulse-light, voice resonance, body markings, and growth geometry. They are not exposed batteries or collectible components. Removing one from a living body is normally fatal or identity-destroying.

### Cradles

Cradles provide the controlled heat, minerals, cultures, signal environment, and social contact needed for repair, new growth, and some births. Wild colonies build ecological cradle-nests from local material; human communities maintain public service cradles; Meridian licenses high-capacity and research cradles.

Society understands inputs, diagnostics, and safe operating ranges. The deepest fabrication language remains inherited and partly opaque. This gap makes origin claims and root access politically important.

### Reweaving

Evolution is called **Reweaving** in technical contexts, though ordinary speech also uses “turning,” “growing,” or “taking a new shape.” The seedcore reorganizes the body after accumulated experience, a changed role, environmental pressure, or a deliberate choice at a cradle. Memory and personality should remain continuous.

Forced or licensed Reweaves exist in industry and are ethically contested. They are a later-story pressure, not a player customization system in the vertical slice.

## Wildkin in daily life

Wildkin were shaped by old service lineages but are not born employees. They are fully intelligent autonomous synthetic beings who reason, plan, learn, remember, disagree, communicate intentionally, understand technical systems, and make voluntary Accord decisions. A Thrumble may map signal paths, pollinate an orchard, follow a musician, debate a route, or refuse every assigned task. Communities function best when they design work around preference and consent.

Many Wildkin communicate more literally, directly, and with less reliance on implied social context than humans. They may be exceptional technical or environmental thinkers while finding sarcasm, hints, euphemism, unspoken emotional subtext, or social ritual unintuitive. This is a cognitive tendency with substantial individual variation, not inferior intelligence.

Visible slice examples:

- Thrumble swarms pollinate bellfruit and carry a local mesh timing reference.
- Latchlings inspect cable hardware, collect access tokens, and imitate security challenges.
- Loamlets rebuild soil beds, seal erosion gaps, and cultivate useful microbes.
- Reedimps monitor water pressure through rhythm.
- Sootnewts store and redistribute waste heat inside service ducts.
- Vellumoths lift degraded pigments and data traces from old surfaces.
- Rucklets release structural strain before walls fail.
- Mirrorminnows inspect dark fluid channels through reflected telemetry.

Households often include resting niches, charge gardens, mineral baths, and interface surfaces scaled for different species. These details should make partnership ordinary before the plot questions who wrote the oldest rules.

### Repair, fabrication, and field professions

Wildkin care is not divided neatly between veterinarian and mechanic. Community cradle workers cultivate tissue, fabricate compatible supports, read behavior, and maintain safe interfaces. Route mechanics repair civic systems in the same material language. Wayfinders add field ecology, negotiation, and certification authority.

The protagonist begins as a cradle-repair apprentice and field systems mechanic seeking Wayfinder certification. This is a recognized, useful career path—not a cover for unique powers. What is unusual is the time they invested in one seedcore that every registry classified as inert.

A **Wildkin Tuner**, normally shortened to **Tuner**, is a person who forms Accord partnerships and coordinates a selected Wildkin lineup in field and battle contexts. Wayfinders, technicians, rivals, and ordinary residents may also be Tuners; Tuner is a relationship-and-practice role, not a separate school-age institution or ownership title.

## Field Data synchronization

Wayfinder and Tuner field systems record battle telemetry and translate it into **Field Data** that bonded Wildkin can inspect and integrate. Each opposing Wildkin generates a configured Data packet when it becomes Spent; those packets accumulate through sequential replacements. After a qualifying victory, the complete accumulated pool synchronizes to every Wildkin in the currently selected battle lineup, including reserves that never became active. The packet is not divided because each recipient receives the same information rather than a share of a physical resource.

This supports Levels without implying that combat experience belongs only to the creature that dealt damage. Synchronization and Level processing happen after victory, not between opponents. Wildkin outside the selected lineup receive no automatic synchronization under the current rule. Refuge catch-up, participation bonuses, reserve penalties, and Reweaving thresholds remain open design points.

## Wild Coins

The canonical currency name is **Wild Coins**, with **Wild Coin** used for exactly one. A victorious Tuner encounter may transfer an encounter-defined Wild Coin reward through the Wayfinder's ordinary account system. Wild Encounters do not normally produce Wild Coins. Defeat and Retreat produce neither normal Field Data synchronization nor Wild Coins.

## The Chorus

The Chorus is the name for the interlocking network by which water, roots, seedcores, wildkin calls, public relays, seasonal movement, and inherited infrastructure exchange timing and route information. It is simultaneously an ecosystem, a communications lattice, and a distributed control substrate. People disagree about whether it also possesses memory or intention beyond its participants.

In play, the Chorus is communicated through:

- layered ambient calls;
- small synchronized motions in plants, light-guides, water, and grown circuitry;
- recurring three-note and five-note local phrases;
- wildkin following recognizable routes;
- the Wayglass showing route histories, link handshakes, and biological traces at authored Survey points.

The game should avoid treating the Chorus as magic-shaped wireless internet. Its signals propagate through material, living carriers, relays, and learned behavior, which is why routes and local maintenance matter. It is powerful because many ordinary relationships reinforce it.

## The Hush

The Hush is a disruption where Chorus links are muted and seedcores fall back to older cached routines. It resembles silence because connected systems stop acknowledging the present.

### Sensory signs

- Ambient layers drop away in a brief radius.
- Water ripples at a delayed or inverted interval.
- Plants, soil, shells, and walls develop pale concentric **checksum rings** where grown material restarts an earlier pattern.
- Wildkin repeat obsolete service actions, reverse a route, fail to recognize a current partner, or answer a command nobody openly sent.
- Screens and friendly public interfaces briefly display legacy symbols before blanking.
- A faint inverted three-note **root handshake** may be felt and seen as a low screen-edge shimmer.

Sound clues always receive a visual counterpart. The mystery cannot depend on the player’s hearing or audio settings.

### Gameplay signs

- Encounter groups use unusual compositions, repeated techniques, or obsolete behavioral loops.
- Survey reveals broken links, doubled route histories, and implausibly old behavior timestamps.
- Familiar field interactions produce changed results.
- Hushbound story creatures require an environmental or Tune solution, not ordinary Accord.

### Design boundary

The Hush is not a universal corruption skin, computer virus, or excuse for chrome monster variants. Its defining horror is loss of present identity: a beloved creature becoming efficient at a task it has not performed in decades, or accepting an authorization its current self never granted. Overusing aggressive “Hush forms” would flatten that idea into a standard monster plague.

## Waybells and Waymarks

### Waybells

“Waybell” is a broad term for open local relays that express a Chorus protocol through sound, light, vibration, or moving water: hanging shells, bridge cables, ceramic plates, reed arrays, or actual bells. Their friendly output lets people and wildkin verify what a system is asking. They coordinate rather than command. A useful waybell amplifies an agreed route; a malicious or badly tuned one can impersonate trust.

### Waymarks

A Waymark is both a surveyed route record and the practical recognition that a local disruption has been understood well enough to travel safely. Waymarks may be held in a Wayglass and copied to a station archive.

They replace badge-like progression because each record can represent a different kind of accomplishment: rescue, negotiation, ecological repair, public contest, or discovery.

## Stillpoints and the Meridian Office

The **Meridian Office** is a regional continuity, safety, and public-works institution expanded after the Red Wake. It certifies cradles, standardizes route and seedcore records, maintains major channels and transit crossings, operates Stillpoints, and holds the legal authority to use several classes of emergency root credential.

A **Stillpoint** holds local Chorus traffic and connected seedcores to a verified “safe” state. This can prevent destructive synchronization cascades, structural resonance, and mass wildkin panic. It can also suppress adaptation, overwrite recent behavior, and push desynchronization into neighboring routes. Most citizens know about stabilization; very few know how deep a rollback can reach.

### Public perception

- Some communities credit Meridian with keeping transit, cradles, and companion networks safe.
- Some wardens and wildkin advocates resent sealed interfaces, revoked local credentials, and manuals that erase contextual practices.
- Most people hold mixed views because they use Meridian infrastructure, remember its rescues, and suspect it claims authority over systems nobody truly owns.
- Field employees range from public servants to institutional loyalists; there are no uniform “grunts” in the slice.

### Why this institution

A continuity authority connects the central conflict to daily comfort, creature welfare, and public safety. Destroying it would strand real communities; leaving its hidden control untouched may be equally irresponsible.

## Factions and social pressure

### Meridian Office

Believes incompatible local systems and unverified Reweaves can trigger another Red Wake. Its best members protect the public; its worst habit is treating secrecy and root authority as neutral technical necessities. Meridian is the only faction directly represented by a central slice character.

### The Hearthworks Cooperative

A loose network of local technicians, growers, route crews, and wildkin partners. Hearthworks favors inspectable systems, community cradles, and protocols that creatures can visibly accept or refuse. Tavi and Edda represent different generations of this approach. The Cooperative is effective locally but inconsistent across regions.

### The Open Chorus

A provisional later-game faction of researchers, rights advocates, escaped industrial wildkin caretakers, and protocol breakers who want seedcore standards and root credentials made public. Some are principled reformers; some release dangerous tools without understanding the infrastructure they affect. In the slice they exist only as a scrubbed symbol or rumor, avoiding faction overload.

### Independent wildkin communities

Many wildkin form colonies around old cradles, derelict transit systems, or habitats they have redesigned. They are not a unified faction and should not be written as waiting for human leadership. Later arcs can involve communities whose goals conflict with both Meridian and local settlements.

### Why these factions

They create disagreements about safety, transparency, personhood, and inherited technology without dividing the cast into “science” and “nature.” Every side uses technology; the differences concern consent, access, risk, and control.

## Historical pressure: the Red Wake

Roughly twenty years before the game, an uncontrolled synchronization event moved through the Merefold. Transit systems rerouted, water reversed through several channels, public cradles activated without operators, and normally independent wildkin converged on obsolete service sites. Some bonded creatures lost recent memories; others Rewove into forms no registry contained. The official account describes an emergent Chorus cascade that Meridian’s early continuity network helped end.

Survivors remember contradictions: an inverted root handshake before the first surge, devices accepting credentials from no visible source, missing time in public logs, and local open protocols disabled immediately before failures. The exact deeper truth remains unresolved, but the new direction favors a Red Wake connected to control architecture rather than a purely natural disaster.

The Red Wake is present through characters and altered systems, not sepia flashback dumps in the vertical slice.

## Hearthmere

### Identity

A compact, bright service town built where orchard channels meet an elevated cable loop. Ceramic displays turn water flow into color and sound; walls carry public mesh diagrams; wildkin-sized paths weave through markets and homes. Hearthmere’s annual Return is part pollination season, part network resynchronization, part maintenance day, and part festival.

### Play spaces

- Arrival square and cable station.
- Compact refuge/waypost with an open community cradle.
- Open Return relay and prepared festival tables.
- View toward the old weir, establishing geography.
- Two small optional interiors at most; essential services stay accessible outside.

### Before/after contrast

Before Orralume, festival objects feel abandoned mid-preparation and villagers watch the route. Afterward, the same space hosts a restrained dusk celebration. NPC positions, ambient layers, flowers, and one wildkin path change.

### Story role

Hearthmere demonstrates why people choose stability. The town is technically capable and affectionate toward wildkin; its harvest, transport, communications, and water safety depend on systems that are now in conflict.

Skein is visually unusual but does not stop daily life. Most residents read its visible repairs as a talented apprentice’s nonstandard local reconstruction; only specialized diagnostics expose the deeper contradiction.

## Sunken Track

### Identity

An old service road lowered over generations by runoff, buried conduit, and repair, with field walls at shoulder height. It acts as habitat corridor and exposed network spine because roots, drainage gaps, warm charge-stones, and obsolete relay pockets create many small niches.

### Exploration language

- Upper rims show destinations before paths reach them.
- Creature silhouettes and visible service behaviors signal habitat branches.
- Broken field-wall gaps become Rig shortcuts.
- Checksum rings, reversed tracks, and legacy interface flashes grow more common toward the weir.
- Visible encounter groups patrol short ecological loops, not random wandering boxes.

### Story role

The route turns “the swarm is missing” into a specific deduction: it arrived, encountered the inverted interval, and returned along its own path.

## Bellwether Orchard

### Identity

Terraced bellfruit trees grown around conductive support lattices. Their hollow seed cups store charge and resonance when pollinated, supplying food and biodegradable sensor material. Tuned shell-relays along the watercourse help Thrumble maintain both their flight route and the orchard mesh through gorge mist.

### Exploration and interaction

- Tune two shells by comparing visible pulse spacing, not by guessing pitches.
- Follow gray Orralume checksum scales and an old service identifier into a utility channel.
- Open one Latchling shortcut by returning the relay key or providing a harmless patterned practice token.
- Observe blossoms opening after the climax.

### Story role

The orchard makes the migration economically and technologically real and places Edda’s open legacy protocols beside Meridian’s sealed instrumentation.

## Old Weir Exterior

### Identity

A layered water-control and data-routing site patched across decades. Stone channels, ceramic logic plates, living seals, and bright community repairs sit beneath a mobile Stillpoint connected by clean blue-white fiber.

### Story role

The site must visually prove two facts at once: the old weir is genuinely dangerous, and the new system is functionally compatible but politically sealed from its local ecology. Merrit’s early rollback prevents falling masonry, a channel breach, and panicked service wildkin on-screen.

## Weirworks

### Identity

A compact utility dungeon with a loop rather than a corridor chain: valve hall, dry bypass, thermal exchange duct, maintenance archive, legacy cradle chamber, and a descending service gallery.

### Traversal

- Survey pressure and handshake traces to choose which bypass to open.
- Use Tune on an old open-protocol plate after finding its missing authorization interval.
- Rig one maintenance bridge as a return shortcut.
- Encounter Sootnewt near heat, Mirrorminnow in dark channels, and Vellumoth in the archive.

### Story role

The dungeon is the argument. Its rooms show that community-readable protocol and precise engineered records each contain half the usable solution. A sealed layer beneath both hints that neither group authored the system they claim to understand.

## Resonance Cistern

### Identity

A broad circular chamber below the weir where water, transit supports, Chorus routing plates, and an ancient industrial cradle meet. Orralume’s reflected wing-map briefly aligns with floor channels and displays a system topology far larger than the Merefold during the final Tune.

### Scope rule

This is the slice’s spectacle space. It earns a unique backdrop, altered lighting, and boss staging. It does not need bespoke cinematic camera systems.

## Exploration reward hierarchy

1. New wildkin behavior, personality, daily role, or team option.
2. A clue that changes interpretation of the Chorus, a seedcore, or an institution.
3. A shortcut or restored public service that respects return travel.
4. A useful technique, diagnostic, or refill.
5. Character perspective, bestiary observation, or social detail about synthetic life.
6. Wild Coins, only when the economy has meaningful purchases.

An optional branch should preferably combine two levels. Pure filler chests are not part of the world identity.

## Future-region seeds, not commitments

These show how the same premise could vary without becoming a biome checklist:

- **Kiln Ridge:** Advanced fabrication districts share waste heat with Ember wildkin; a major battle concerns an industrial Reweave quota and a failing thermal route during cold weather.
- **Saltstep Terraces:** Solar transit paths cross mirrored flats whose surface hides old Waymarks; a public race doubles as a dispute over whether Meridian can revoke independent wildkin route credentials.
- **Canopy Archive:** Vellumoth colonies preserve damaged records in living wing patterns while researchers debate whether reading them also overwrites them.
- **Floodplain Ferries:** Semi-autonomous ferry wildkin and moving habitat platforms create routes that change through authored story states rather than a dynamic open world.
- **The Buried Cradle:** A sealed fabrication complex contains body plans for species nobody remembers and handshake records that predate current human settlement.

Only locations that produce a distinct local conflict and gameplay rhythm should advance into full design.

## Worldbuilding delivery rules

- Show a technology making life better before revealing its control layer.
- Use consistent materials and repair styles to imply age.
- Give optional NPCs conflicting, concrete experiences.
- Repeat mystery motifs across sound, interface behavior, body growth, animation, object placement, and creature routines.
- Do not put critical facts only in bestiary or archive text.
- Avoid proper-noun density; introduce at most one important term in a short routine scene.
- Let post-climax map changes answer “what did that accomplish?” without dialogue.
- Never use synthetic origin as shorthand for emotional falseness. A Wildkin's attachment and speech are real even if a protocol can interfere with memory or communication.
- Let Wildkin participate in dialogue when personality and context warrant it; do not turn every wild encounter into a conversation or write every Wildkin with the same literal voice.

## World playtest questions

1. Can players draw the opening route from memory after finishing?
2. Does Hearthmere feel like an appealing high-technology town dependent on wildkin partnership rather than a rustic village with gadgets?
3. Do players notice the Hush before a character defines it?
4. Are community-open and Meridian-sealed systems visually distinct at map scale without making one look primitive?
5. Does Weirworks feel like a place with a function rather than a themed dungeon?
6. Which optional environmental clue produces the strongest theory?
7. Can players explain seedcores, cradles, and Reweaving without a lore glossary?
8. Do they perceive Wildkin as fully intelligent synthetic persons with non-human cognitive tendencies, rather than animals or equipment?
9. Do Tuner partnership and Field Data synchronization feel like world practices rather than game-only terminology?

## Decisions awaiting approval

- The Merefold as a connected, colorful high-technology orchard/wetland basin.
- Warm civic science fantasy as the visual register: advanced, tactile, repairable, and social rather than sterile.
- Wildkin as living synthetic beings grown around seedcores and integrated into ordinary work and family life.
- The Chorus as inherited ecological communications and control infrastructure, not a speaking deity.
- The Hush as behavioral rollback and identity loss rather than generic corruption or a simple virus.
- Meridian as both public works and continuity authority, with legal root access shaped by the Red Wake.
- Hearthworks, the Open Chorus, and independent wildkin communities as provisional future pressures rather than immediate exposition.
- A tightly connected Hearthmere route instead of an open-world slice.
- “Wildkin Tuner,” shortened to “Tuner,” as the canonical role name for bonded lineup coordinators.
- Field Data as per-opponent telemetry pooled when opposing Wildkin become Spent, then synchronized in full to every selected lineup member after victory; Tuner victories may additionally grant encounter-configured Wild Coins.
- Wildkin as fully intelligent and verbal, with a typically literal/low-context but individually varied social model.
- Skein's comparative anomaly as stronger emotional/social inference, not unique speech.
